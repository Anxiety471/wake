# Code of conduct

wake is a small tool maintained in public. Everyone who files an issue, opens a pull
request or comments is expected to keep it that way: direct about the code, never about
the person.

## Expected

- Assume the other person is trying to make the tool better.
- Critique the change, not the author. "This drops the token on an error path" is
  useful; "you are careless" is not.
- Keep reports reproducible: what you ran, what happened, what you expected.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or sustained disruption in issues, pull requests or reviews.
- Discrimination based on who someone is, on any ground.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission: real names,
  addresses, keys, hostnames, or patient and institutional data.
- Bringing a dispute from somewhere else into this issue tracker.

## Reporting

Do not argue on the thread. Report it privately:

- **Conduct:** use GitHub's [Report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse)
  form and name this repository and the account involved.
- **A vulnerability, or a secret pasted into an issue:** use private vulnerability
  reporting as described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). Never repeat the secret in
  another issue or comment.

Reports are handled privately. The person reported is not told who reported them.

## Consequences

An issue, comment or review that breaks this may be edited or deleted. An account that
keeps at it may be blocked from the repository. A second look by the maintainer is the
only appeal.

## Scope

This applies in the repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews) and in
public places where someone is speaking for this project.
